\title{A Policy-Indexed Method for Canonical Reconstruction and Fail-Closed
Delivery of Untrusted Media}

\author{Oleksandr Melnychenko\texorpdfstring{\,\orcidlink{0000-0001-8565-7092}}{}\texorpdfstring{\corref{cor1}}{}}
\ead{melnychenko@khmnu.edu.ua}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address{Khmelnytskyi National University, Khmelnytskyi, Ukraine}

\begin{abstract}
Authenticated channels may deliver ambiguous media unsuitable for local use.
We define a transport-independent method that reconciles filename, declared
type, and bounded byte evidence; applies policy-required reconstruction;
validates the candidate independently; and publishes only clean,
non-overwriting output. The method
addresses type confusion, ambiguous structures, retained codec payloads,
resource amplification, and publication faults across image, audio, ISO base
media, and WebM profiles. The analysis gives conditional canonical-delivery and
fixed-witness post-reconstruction monotonicity theorems under
authorizing-parser soundness. A bounded experiment used 104 generated benign
or inert-unsupported records. Fixture-derived action oracles matched, measuring
fixture-policy consistency rather than independent ground truth. Eighty
accepted evaluations produced 40 unique outputs that passed at least two
applicable external checks. A later versioned readiness cycle, without relaxed
thresholds, recorded 80/80 fidelity and differential checks, 312/312
paired-ablation rows, 14/14 fault cases, and 6/6 available byte-identical
regeneration checks. Its 129 generated rehearsal records spanned S0--S7 but did
not meet the frozen protocol budgets. Public-source discovery retained 316
candidates, yet no independent signature was received and 0/246 sources were
admitted. A shared host, 0/21 fuzz target-hours, and 0/10 controlled
performance sessions kept \(G_{\mathrm{sci}}\) and release eligibility false;
the observations authorize no release, population, malware-detection, or
capacity claim.
\end{abstract}
