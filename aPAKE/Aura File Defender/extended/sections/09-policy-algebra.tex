\section{Policy Algebra and Fixed-Witness Monotonicity}
\label{sec:policy-algebra}

\subsection{Extensional policy representation}

A named profile such as strict or paranoid is not itself a mathematical policy.
For comparison, decision-relevant content is represented extensionally as
\begin{equation}
  p=(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{T},\mathcal{G},q,
  \mathcal{S},\mathcal{M},\mathbf{b}^{\max},\mathbf{b}^{\min},v),
  \label{eq:policy-tuple}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{K}$ is the admitted profile set, $\mathcal{N}$ is the accepted
name language, $\mathcal{T}$ is the admitted type and alias relation,
$\mathcal{G}=\{L_{p,k}\}$ is the family of canonical output languages, $q$ is
the reconstruction minimum, $\mathcal{S}$ is the signature rejection relation,
$\mathcal{M}$ is the metadata prohibition relation, and the two typed vectors
contain maximum and minimum resource constraints. Registry version $v$ fixes
the complete-parser observation set $\mathcal{O}_v$.

The policy tuple contains no coordinate for deliverable warning or error states.
Clean-only publication is a method invariant. A diagnostic configuration that
exposes warning output is outside the scientific acceptance relation rather
than a less restrictive member of this algebra.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Coordinate-wise definition of a policy restriction. Parser
observations remain fixed so that restriction cannot hide negative evidence.}
\label{tab:policy-coordinates}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{L{0.22\textwidth}YY}
\toprule
Policy coordinate & Condition for $p_2$ to be no more permissive than $p_1$ & Effect on candidate validation \\
\midrule
Profiles and names & $\mathcal{K}_2\subseteq\mathcal{K}_1$ and $\mathcal{N}_2\subseteq\mathcal{N}_1$ & Removes routes or admitted names \\
Type and alias relation & $\mathcal{T}_2\subseteq\mathcal{T}_1$ under one registry normalization & Removes declarations or compatibilities \\
Canonical languages & $L_{p_2,k}\subseteq L_{p_1,k}$ for every common $k$ & Removes admitted output structures \\
Reconstruction level & $q_{p_1}(k)\preceq q_{p_2}(k)$ & Requires equal or stronger work \\
Signatures and metadata & $\mathcal{S}_1\subseteq\mathcal{S}_2$ and $\mathcal{M}_1\subseteq\mathcal{M}_2$ & Adds rejection conditions \\
Maximum limits & $\mathbf{b}^{\max}_2\leq\mathbf{b}^{\max}_1$ coordinate-wise & Narrows upper resource bounds \\
Minimum requirements & $\mathbf{b}^{\min}_2\geq\mathbf{b}^{\min}_1$ coordinate-wise & Raises required evidence or quality floors \\
Parser registry & $v_2=v_1$ and hence $\mathcal{O}_{v_2}=\mathcal{O}_{v_1}$ & Preserves all complete observations \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Only bounds with the same unit and interpretation are comparable. A maximum
duration cannot be ordered against a minimum sample rate, and heterogeneous
limits are never collapsed into one score.

\begin{definition}[Policy restriction order]
\label{def:policy-order}
We write $p_2\sqsubseteq p_1$ when every coordinate in
\cref{tab:policy-coordinates} is no more permissive in $p_2$ than in $p_1$.
Thus $p_2$ is at least as restrictive as $p_1$.
\end{definition}

Under extensional equality, $\sqsubseteq$ is a partial order. Reflexivity follows
from coordinate equality. Antisymmetry follows from mutual inclusion and equal
typed bounds. Transitivity follows from set inclusion, the reconstruction-level
chain, and the ordinary orders on comparable maxima and minima. Distinct
serialized documents can still denote the same extensional policy.

\subsection{Fixed-witness validation monotonicity}

Let $z=(a,c,k)$ fix the input, candidate, selected profile, parser reports, and
all evidence in $c.\eta$. Define $\mathsf{Val}_p(z)$ as the post-route
conjunction in \cref{eq:extended-acceptance}, omitting
$\mathsf{Route}_p(a)=k$. It does not invoke a route, decoder, encoder, or
reconstruction computation.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-witness validation monotonicity]
\label{prop:validation-monotonicity}
For a fixed witness $z$ under one registry version, if
$p_2\sqsubseteq p_1$ and $\mathsf{Val}_{p_2}(z)$, then
$\mathsf{Val}_{p_1}(z)$. Equivalently, a fixed post-route witness rejected by
$p_1$ cannot pass unchanged under the more restrictive $p_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Analytical argument]
A name, profile, declaration, and canonical structure admitted by $p_2$ is also
admitted by $p_1$. Satisfaction of the stronger reconstruction minimum implies
satisfaction of the weaker minimum. Passing the larger signature and metadata
rejection relations implies passing their subsets. A value below each smaller
maximum is below the corresponding larger maximum. A value above each larger
minimum is above the corresponding smaller minimum. Both policies inspect the
same $\mathsf{Full}_v(c.y)$, so restriction cannot remove an incompatible
complete parse. Every conjunct required by $p_1$ therefore holds for the fixed
witness.
\end{proof}

The qualification ``fixed witness'' is essential. A weaker policy can admit
additional routes and thereby make its pre-dispatch route set ambiguous. This
case is outside the post-route proposition. A stricter policy can also choose
a different reconstruction requirement and produce a different candidate. A
structural candidate rejected under $p_1$ can be replaced by a semantic
candidate accepted under $p_2$. This is not acceptance of the same witness and
does not contradict the proposition. Consequently, this manuscript makes no
unconditional end-to-end monotonicity claim across policies that alter routing,
decoder, encoder, canonicalization, or output profile.

Paired policy evaluation therefore separates two questions. The first asks
whether one fixed candidate remains rejected under restriction. The second
records whether a policy change creates a different candidate and must be
interpreted as a reconstruction change. A generated policy diff identifies the
changed coordinate and the candidate digests. It cannot silently classify a new
repair path as a monotonicity success.

\subsection{Restrictive meet}

For policies over the same registry version, $p_1\sqcap p_2$ intersects positive
admission sets and canonical languages, takes the stronger reconstruction
minimum, unions signature and metadata prohibitions, takes the smaller
applicable maximum, and takes the larger applicable minimum. Its version record
contains both parent digests and the deterministic composition rule.

\begin{proposition}[Greatest lower bound]
\label{prop:policy-meet}
When profile semantics and typed bound domains are compatible,
$p_1\sqcap p_2$ is the greatest policy under $\sqsubseteq$ that is no more
permissive than both parents.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Analytical argument]
Every meet coordinate is a lower bound by construction. Any policy $r$ no more
permissive than both parents admits only their shared positive sets, requires at
least their stronger reconstruction level, enforces their combined
prohibitions, uses maxima no greater than either parent, and uses minima no
smaller than either parent. Hence $r\sqsubseteq p_1\sqcap p_2$. If registry
versions or bound meanings differ, the meet is undefined until both policies
are migrated to one frozen registry.
\end{proof}

The permissive join is not used to construct release policy. Unioning languages
can create combinations that were never evaluated. A relaxation is a new policy
version with new evidence. Scientific claims bind to the serialized policy,
alias table, canonical registry, implementation commit, and their hashes.
